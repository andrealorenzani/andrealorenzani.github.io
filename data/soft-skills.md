## People-first leadership
icon: 🤝

### Details
I believe people do their best work when they're trusted, not supervised. At Charlotte Tilbury I led three engineering squads at once — C3PO, ABS and Horizon, about 11 engineers inside a wider 18–20 person community. That's a lot of stand-ups, and the thing I'm proudest of isn't a system I built — it's that team engagement kept climbing wherever I went (TEG: OfficeVibe 7.2 → 8.6).

### Examples
- Coached Product Managers and Delivery Managers through Black Friday stress instead of letting them white-knuckle it alone.
- Helped two engineers build the confidence to present at our technical forum.
- Noticed a quieter teammate going silent during a stressful incident — pulled them into the conversation, then followed up properly over lunch afterwards.

## Open door, always
icon: 🚪

### Details
My door — or Slack DM — is open to anyone: my own team, QAs, Product, anyone who's stuck. I'd rather answer a "silly" question at 4pm than debug the consequences of it at 4am.

### Examples
- Usually the first person QAs and PMs pinged when something looked off, because I'd rather look into it than let it quietly become someone else's Friday.
- Ran a Squad Health Check at MetaPack specifically because getting honest feedback from people who report to you is hard — found "learning" was a weak spot, and fixed it with Friday hackathons.

## Data-driven
icon: 📊

### Details
I don't walk into a stakeholder conversation without numbers. Gather the evidence, then act — not the other way round.

### Examples
- Proved tracking-event delays with hard data before asking anyone to reprioritise a roadmap at MetaPack.
- Proved Customer Care call volumes with data before touching a single line of the order flow at Charlotte Tilbury.

---

## Direct, not diplomatic-to-a-fault
icon: 🎯

### Details
I say the thing, professionally. I'd rather have one slightly awkward conversation than three vague ones.

### Examples
- Pushed back on delivering PayPal v2 right before Black Friday, even though my manager and the PMs wanted it. Made the case with the actual risk numbers — they came round, and Black Friday went smoothly.

---

## Fail fast, on purpose
icon: 🧪

### Details
Build small, test early, and be willing to throw it away if it's wrong. A rejected idea that took two days is a much better outcome than a "perfect" one that took two months.

### Examples
- Built the FTP Poller at MetaPack as a deliberately scrappy, fail-fast prototype rather than a polished first attempt.
- Built a promo-stacking POC at Charlotte Tilbury that Sales rejected for being too complex — instead of arguing, I just built something simpler.

---

## Curious
icon: 🔍

### Details
I genuinely enjoy learning new things, not just for the CV line. It's usually why the interesting projects happen in the first place.

### Examples
- Learned AWS Athena and data lakes on a MetaPack side project, then reused that exact skill years later at Charlotte Tilbury for a completely different problem.
- Picked up OpenTelemetry because I wanted to actually see what was happening in production, not guess.

## Patient, and a genuinely good listener
icon: 👂

### Details
This one comes with an honest story: a colleague once gave me feedback that I interrupt people. I wasn't 100% sure it was always fair — but I took it seriously anyway, and I've been consciously leaving more space for others to finish speaking ever since.

### Examples
- Actively works to let others speak first in meetings, especially quieter voices.
- Known on every team as patient, approachable, and open to criticism — not just my own description, feedback I've had repeatedly.

---

## Quality-obsessed
icon: 🛡️

### Details
Testing and observability aren't optional extras — they're the foundation everything else is built on.

### Examples
- Introduced API tests, load testing and integration testing as standard practice at both MetaPack and Charlotte Tilbury.
- Introduced a "Clean as you Code" rule at TEG that nobody has dared break yet.
